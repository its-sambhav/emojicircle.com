Emoji Timeline: The Evolution of Emojis from 1988 to 2026 🗓️

✍️ By Sambhav Jain 📅 Published August 2026 ⏱ 7 min read 🏷 #EmojiHistory #EmojiTimeline
⚡ The short version Emoji have three distinct eras. 1988–1999: pictograph sets on Japanese organisers and phones, incompatible with each other. 2010: Unicode absorbs them, and emoji become a standard rather than a product feature. 2015–today: a versioned release cycle that has settled into one dated update every September.

Most emoji histories are essays. This one is a reference. Below is every emoji release we can date from primary sources, in order, with the exact day it shipped — because "emoji were invented in 1999" is the kind of tidy sentence that hides about a decade of prior art and fifteen years of versioning.

Era one: the pictograph years, 1988–1999

Before emoji were a standard, they were a hardware feature — a small grid of symbols baked into a specific device, usable only by people who owned the same model.

1988
Sharp PA-8500, October 1988 — an electronic organiser carrying over 100 emoji-like designs at 16×16 pixels. The earliest such set currently documented.[2]
1990
NEC PI-ET1, August 1990 — over 130 emoji-like designs, also drawn at 16×16 pixels.[2]
1994
Sharp Zaurus PI-4000, June 1994 — roughly 200 symbols at 12×12 pixels, building on the 1988 set.[2]
1997
SoftBank SkyWalker DP-211SW, 1 November 1997 — 90 distinct emoji characters, and the first mobile phone to carry an emoji set. It sold poorly and the emoji worked on no other handset.[1][2]
1999
NTT DoCoMo i-mode, January 1999 — Shigetaka Kurita's set of 176 emoji. Not the first, but the one that reached enough people to make emoji an expectation.[1][2]

That last distinction is contested often enough that we gave it its own page: what was the first emoji, and why the answer keeps moving.

Era two: 11 October 2010, when emoji became a standard

The turning point in the timeline is 11 October 2010. That is the date attached to emoji version 0.6 in Unicode's own emoji specification, UTS #51, and it corresponds to Unicode 6.0.[3] From that point on, an emoji was no longer a picture your carrier drew — it was a codepoint every vendor agreed to render.

Why that mattered mechanically, and why the same codepoint still looks different on an iPhone and a Pixel, is the subject of our guide to how Unicode makes emoji work everywhere.

Era three: every emoji version and its release date

Seventeen dated emoji releases sit between 2010 and 2025. These dates come from the Modifications section of UTS #51, the specification Unicode maintains for emoji itself.[3]

Two patterns fall straight out of that list. The early years were erratic — two releases in 2015, two in 2016, then nothing at all in 2013. And since Emoji 14.0, the cycle has locked: 14 September 2021, 13 September 2022, 12 September 2023, 10 September 2024, 9 September 2025. Five consecutive September releases, each a day or two earlier in the month than the last.[3]

The gap in the numbers: why 5.0 is followed by 11.0

If you scan the list you'll notice emoji versions 6 through 10 don't exist. This is the single most confusing thing about the emoji timeline, and the explanation is in the specification itself. UTS #51 states that starting with version 11.0, the repertoire of emoji characters is synchronized with the Unicode Standard and uses the same version numbering system.[3]

Emoji 5.0 shipped alongside Unicode 10.0. Rather than let the two numbers drift further apart, the next emoji release skipped ahead to 11.0 to match Unicode 11.0 — and they have moved in lockstep ever since. So an "Emoji 8.0" was never cancelled or withdrawn. It simply never existed.

📌 Reading the timeline correctly: a release date is the day Unicode approved the set, not the day it reached your keyboard. Those are separate events, often a year apart. We track the second one on our platform-by-platform rollout page.

Where the timeline stands today

Emoji 17.0, released 9 September 2025, is the current version. It added 7 new emoji codepoints — Distorted Face, Fight Cloud, Orca, Hairy Creature, Trombone, Landslide and Treasure Chest — bringing the total number of Unicode-recommended emoji to 3,953.[4]

The next entry is already drafted. Unicode publishes the following batch as a beta chart, Emoji Recently Added, v18.0β, listing nine emoji: Cracking Face, Leftwards Thumb Sign, Rightwards Thumb Sign, Monarch Butterfly, Pickle, Lighthouse, Meteor, Eraser and Net with Handle.[5] Counting the ten additional two-skin-tone modifier variants, that is 19 provisional candidates, expected to be approved in September 2026 and to take the total to 3,972.[6]

Nothing on that beta chart is final until approval. We keep the full breakdown, with what changed between drafts, on our Emoji 17.0 and 18.0 guide.

💬 EmojiCircle's Take

Laid out end to end, the emoji timeline is really a story about governance. The first eleven years were companies building incompatible symbol sets; the last fifteen have been a committee shipping a dated release every September, with the batch sizes shrinking as the obvious gaps get filled. It's less romantic than the one-inventor version — and far easier to verify.
